The thesis

Why people would switch from Ren'Py

Ren'Py can make every game on this page. Its authors do it with the screen language, Python, and community add-ons. A search for a Ren'Py phone system finds at least six on itch.io (Kesash, kleineluka, Alphonsinious, badmustard, elckarow, Nighten). Each is set up its own way. One says its older version is not fully compatible with the new one; another has an ImportError report on Ren'Py 8.2. Calendars, inventories and dating-sim kits are the same: LemonadeMochi, Psykhae, PatchMonk, LezInventory, NQTR, and the long-running Dating Sim Engine.

That is the opening. Authors go looking for these systems one add-on at a time. We can ship them inside the engine, done once and done right.

1Genre systems come built in

Declared in plain script, checked at launch with plain-English errors, saved and rolled back correctly, and colored by the 28 color schemes. No add-on to find, install or keep compatible.

2Every system has a Studio form

Script and forms are the same source of truth, so a writer who likes text and a friend who likes forms can share one project. PC Gamer recommends TyranoBuilder to newcomers who don't want to touch code. Ren'Py leans the other way, toward code.

3Players are looked after by default

Timing assist, reduce motion and skippable minigames are already engine-wide. Add read aloud, captions and content controls and accessibility becomes a reason to pick the engine.

Where Ren'Py still wins: a web build (marked beta), phones, Live2D, the screen language for anything custom, and a large, long-running community. We should not fight those first. We make the common genre game easy and correct, then add the browser.

The unifying idea

One mechanic, many skins

The owner's pigeon example is the whole design. A messenger pigeon is the phone chat with a paper skin and a one-day delay. A sanity meter is an affection meter with a different picture. Challenged

Considered and dropped

Each of these came up in research. Each would make the engine bigger without making it easier.

  • A screen language like Ren'Py's. It is Ren'Py's most powerful part and its hardest to learn, and it puts layout in code. Declared skins and Studio forms cover the common cases. Revisit a small "panel" declaration only if authors keep hitting walls.
  • A battle system. RPG Maker exists. Encounters (I-22) give the feel with checks and a meter.
  • Letters between real players. Kind Words needs servers and moderation. The engine never uses the network, and should keep that promise.
  • Live2D or Spine. Visual Novel Maker supports Live2D; it brings licensing and a runtime. Layered sprites, blink and talk frames, and idle life cover most of the effect.
  • Android and iOS. Not before the browser build. A browser build already reaches phones.
  • Real-world clock events. They break rollback and replay. Story time (I-03) does the job.
  • A full Steam bridge. F37's platform hook already covers achievements. Cloud saves and overlays are store-specific; a pack (I-39) can do them later.
  • Gacha, loot boxes, ads. No.
For you

Questions for you

  1. Inbox before or after the hub systems? I ranked it second because it is your idea and the most distinctive. Building Events, Calendar and Places first would make the inbox's delays and deliveries cheaper.
  2. Meters hidden by default? I suggest yes: a meter shows only where the story or [meter.*] asks.
  3. Checks: two dice plus the stat (Disco Elysium's feel), or a plain percent chance?
  4. New words. The proposals add event, events, clock, map, place, goal, complete, check, success, failure, send, ring, present, roll, draw, weather, camera, mood, content and bubble. Should they follow the tip and item rule (words only in their position, still free as variable names)?
  5. Web spike: is one week on pygbag worth it now, or after the first genre systems ship?
  6. Starters: Mystery and Horror first, since they need nothing new?
